What we offer

Consultations

If you are uncertain which type of survey your project requires, or want to understand the surveying and permitting process before committing to a scope of work, we are glad to discuss your situation. A brief conversation can often clarify what is needed and whether a survey is the right next step.

Boundary & Property Surveys

A boundary survey locates or re-establishes the legal limits of a parcel of land using recorded deeds, title documents, existing maps, and physical field measurements. The surveyor researches the chain of title, locates any existing monuments, sets new monuments where needed, and prepares a map showing the boundary lines and their relation to adjoining properties.

In California, certain boundary surveys must be filed with the county recorder as a Record of Survey. Common reasons to commission one include property sales, construction planning, fence installation, and resolving boundary disputes with adjacent owners.

Topographic Surveys

A topographic survey maps the existing conditions of a site — ground elevations, contours, structures, paving, utilities, vegetation, and other physical features. The resulting map gives engineers and architects the accurate site data they need to design grading, drainage, foundations, and building layouts.

Topographic surveys are typically required before pulling a grading permit or beginning site design work. The level of detail in the survey is matched to the needs of the project.

Construction Staking

Construction staking translates an approved set of design plans into physical marks on the ground — stakes, hubs, and offset markers that show construction crews the exact location, alignment, and elevation of structures, grading limits, utility lines, and other improvements.

Staking is typically performed in phases as construction progresses. Accurate field staking ensures work is built in the correct position relative to property lines, setback requirements, and approved permit plans.

ALTA / NSPS Surveys

An ALTA/NSPS Land Title Survey is a boundary survey prepared according to the minimum standard detail requirements jointly adopted by the American Land Title Association (ALTA) and the National Society of Professional Surveyors (NSPS). These standards specify what must be researched, measured, and shown on the survey map.

ALTA surveys document property boundaries, existing improvements, easements, encroachments, access, utilities, and flood zone information. They are most commonly required by lenders and title companies as a condition of commercial real estate transactions.

Lot Line Adjustments & Subdivisions

A lot line adjustment modifies the shared boundary between two or more adjacent, legally created parcels without creating additional lots. Land is transferred from one parcel to another, with the total parcel count remaining the same. Lot line adjustments require county review and approval and must be supported by a licensed surveyor's map and legal descriptions.

A subdivision divides one parcel into two or more separate parcels, each capable of independent conveyance or development. Subdivisions are subject to the Subdivision Map Act and local ordinance, and require preparation of a parcel map or final map by a licensed land surveyor.

Elevation Certificates

An elevation certificate is an official document prepared by a licensed land surveyor that records a structure's lowest floor elevation relative to the Base Flood Elevation (BFE) established by FEMA for that area. The certificate documents the property's flood zone designation, the building's lowest adjacent grade, and other characteristics relevant to flood insurance rating.

Elevation certificates are required to obtain or maintain flood insurance under the National Flood Insurance Program and are frequently required by local building departments for permits on properties located in designated Special Flood Hazard Areas.

Easement Documentation

An easement is a legal right granted to a party to use a defined portion of another's property for a specific purpose — such as access, drainage, or the installation and maintenance of utilities. Easements are created by written instrument, recorded with the county, and run with the land through subsequent ownership changes.

A licensed surveyor prepares the field measurements, plats, and legal descriptions needed to properly define, create, or modify an easement for recording. Accurate documentation is essential to ensure the easement area is clearly identified and legally enforceable.

Condominium Conversions

A condominium plan is a recorded map that defines the boundaries of individual air space units, common areas, and limited common areas within a condominium project. In California, converting an existing multi-unit building to individually owned condominiums requires preparation of a condominium plan by a licensed land surveyor, followed by county review and approval prior to recordation.
